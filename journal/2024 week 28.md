## Week 28 - Energy for CO<sub>2</sub>
### What's required
Let's calculate the amount of energy we need to operate with CO<sub>2</sub>.

Properties of CO<sub>2</sub>:
* Molecular weight: 44.0095 g/mol - [source](https://www.engineeringtoolbox.com/CO2-carbon-dioxide-properties-d_2017.html)
* Gas density: 44.9 mol/m3 - [source](https://www.engineeringtoolbox.com/CO2-carbon-dioxide-properties-d_2017.html)
* Liquid density: 25017 mol/m3 - [source](https://www.engineeringtoolbox.com/CO2-carbon-dioxide-properties-d_2017.html)
* Liquefaction heat: 16.4 kJ/mol - [source](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/349366110_Kerogen_nanoscale_structure_and_CO2_adsorption_in_shale_micropores) - Confirmation required!
* Latent heat of evaporisation (at tripple point): 15.55 kJ/mol - [source](https://www.engineeringtoolbox.com/CO2-carbon-dioxide-properties-d_2017.html)
* Specific heat capacity (isobaric): 37.35 J/mol K - [source](https://www.engineeringtoolbox.com/CO2-carbon-dioxide-properties-d_2017.html)

Energy, required to change temperature of 1 ml of CO<sub>2</sub> from 25 &deg;C to -40 &deg;C:
```
25017 * 37.35 * (25 + 40) / 1e6 = 60.735 J/ml = 0.017 Wh/ml
```

Energy, required to liquefy 1 ml of CO<sub>2</sub>:
```
25017 * 16.4 / 1e6 = 0.410279 kJ/ml = 0.114 Wh/ml
```

Total energy, required to cool down and liquefy 1 ml of CO<sub>2</sub>:
```
0.017 + 0.114 = 0.131 Wh/ml
```

We can also use the latent heat of evaporisation to calculate the liquefaction energy. Its value increases with lower temperature, hence it's maximal at the tripple point:
```
25017e-6 * 15.55 = 0.389 kJ/ml = 0.108 Wh/ml
```
